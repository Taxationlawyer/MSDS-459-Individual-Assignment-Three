## Part Three, Fundamental Analysis Investing

Fundamental analysis involves the assessment of financial ratios (including profitability, efficiency, liquidity, and other measures) to assess the value of the firm and predict the future value of the company—the stock value. Other factors are considered. For example, the firm’s management quality can be considered along with innovative efforts (patent ownership) and investments in research and development. 

The benefits of technical analysis are that an experienced financial expert can use fundamental analysis to predict the future value of a company and make money by investing in profitable ventures; another benefit is that predictions can be used to make operational changes and decisions to keep the firm solvent and profitable. 

The disadvantages of fundamental analysis are that seasoned financial experts are needed to make the assessments; also, these evaluations are highly subjective.

In short, despite the limitations, fundamental analysis can be used in the term project in conjunction with other available resources to make evidence-based and well-reasoned decisions (decisions supported by evidence and human intelligence reasoning).
